Loopinglive · Super admin

The panel you'd want at 1,000 customers

The super admin panel has five pages and answers roughly one question: how much money are we making? It cannot yet answer the question an admin actually asks most days — “this specific customer says something is broken, what happened?” Below is everything I would add, ranked by what it costs versus what it saves.

Five tables ship with zero admin UI: error_logs, feature_flags, affiliates, affiliate_referrals, and the admin_note column. The schema, the writes and the RLS all exist — nothing reads them back. Nobody can currently pay an affiliate, because the payout queue has no screen.

What the panel does today

  • MRR, ARR, total revenue, ARPU, churn, free→paid conversion
  • Revenue and signups by month, plan distribution
  • User list with plan grants, suspension and impersonation
  • Coupon creation, synced to Stripe
  • Platform announcements

Customer support

The largest gap by a distance. When someone emails “my reminders never went out”, there is currently no screen that answers it — you would go to the database. Every hour spent doing that is an hour not spent building.

Answering “what happened to this customer?” Highest value 12 additions
  • Global search One box that resolves an email, name, webinar title, session id, invoice id or registrant address to the right record. The single highest-leverage control on the page.
  • A real user detail page Today a user is a table row and a dropdown. It should be a page: profile, plan history, invoices, webinars, sessions, integrations, API keys, affiliate status.
  • Per-user activity timeline Signed up, published, went live, first registrant, paid, payment failed. Most support questions are answered by reading this in order.
  • Message delivery log per userdata exists Every email, SMS and WhatsApp with its status and the provider's own error string. This is the screen that answers the reminder question directly.
  • Resend a message · retry a webhook Seeing the failure is half of it. Fixing it without a database session is the other half.
  • Issue a refund The 30-day guarantee is a promise in the Terms with no button behind it. Currently it means opening Stripe.
  • Extend a trial · apply account credit The two concessions support makes constantly, and the two that most reliably save a cancellation.
  • Change a customer's email address People mistype it at signup and then cannot receive the confirmation. There is no recovery path today.
  • Trigger a password reset Sends the branded reset rather than telling someone to find the form themselves.
  • Admin notes on an accountcolumn exists admin_note is in the schema and nothing writes or reads it. Context that survives between conversations.
  • GDPR export and erasure Both are promised in the Privacy Policy and neither exists. The first subject access request will be handled by hand.
  • Per-user feature flagstable exists feature_flags is built and unreachable. Turn a beta on for one customer without a deploy.

Platform health

Nothing in the product currently tells you when it is broken. Two failures this week — a cron reading a config key nobody had written, and a bounced address silently suppressed — both looked exactly like health from the outside.

Knowing it is working Blind spot 10 additions
  • Cron monitor All seven pg_cron jobs with last run, duration and outcome. A job that silently stops today is invisible until a customer notices.
  • Error log viewertable exists error_logs receives every caught render failure and nothing displays it. Group by message, show first and last seen, count.
  • Message queue depth Pending and overdue scheduled messages. A backlog is the earliest signal that dispatch has stalled.
  • Webhook delivery healthdata exists Failure rate, and which endpoints are failing repeatedly. Hosts rarely notice their own broken endpoint.
  • Email deliverability Bounce rate, complaint rate, and the suppression list. Exactly what bit us: a suppressed address returns a normal message id and is never delivered.
  • Provider status Are Resend, Twilio, Cloudinary, Stripe and Anthropic reachable, and are their keys still valid? A placeholder key should be visible here, not discovered in production.
  • AI spend Anthropic tokens and cost per host and per webinar. This is real money that scales with usage and is currently unmeasured.
  • Storage and bandwidth by host Cloudinary is the other cost that scales. One host uploading 4K video changes the unit economics.
  • Rate limit hits Who is hitting 429s — a legitimate customer being throttled, or someone probing.
  • Database growth Table sizes and growth rate, so the first capacity problem is a plan rather than an incident.

Business intelligence

What is there is accurate but shallow. MRR tells you the level; none of it tells you the direction or the reason.

Beyond a single MRR figure High 11 additions
  • MRR waterfall New, expansion, contraction, churned, reactivated. “MRR rose 8%” means nothing without knowing which of those moved.
  • Cohort retention Of everyone who joined in a given month, how many are still paying at month 3, 6, 12. The one chart that predicts whether the business works.
  • Activation funnel Signed up → published → first registrant → first sale. Where the drop is tells you what to fix, and it is a leading indicator of everything downstream.
  • Time to value Median days from signup to first published webinar, and to first sale. Shortening this moves retention more than any feature.
  • Trial conversion Started, activated, converted — with the drop-off between them.
  • Net revenue retention · quick ratio The two numbers an investor asks for first, and neither is currently derivable.
  • LTV, and LTV against acquisition cost Decides whether paid acquisition is viable at all.
  • Dunning recovery rate What share of failed payments the sequence actually rescues. Involuntary churn is the cheapest churn to prevent, and unmeasured today.
  • Refund rate A 30-day guarantee is a liability worth watching, per plan and per cohort.
  • Feature adoption Share of hosts using AI personas, SMS, WhatsApp, integrations. Tells you what to invest in and what to retire.
  • Date range control and CSV export Revenue is hard-coded to twelve months and nothing exports. Every table here should export.

Trust and safety

Worth taking seriously precisely because of what this product does. It presents recorded video as live and simulates a crowd — legitimate as a format, and also attractive to people selling things they should not. Your own Terms already prohibit this; nothing enforces it.

Governing what runs on the platform Before scale 8 additions
  • New-host review queue A look at the first webinar before it can publish. Cheap while volume is low, and it sets the tone for who joins.
  • Risky-claim flagging Surface webinars whose copy mentions guaranteed income, medical outcomes or crypto returns. Flag for a human, do not auto-block.
  • Attendee reporting There is currently no way for an attendee to tell you anything is wrong.
  • Suspension reasons and audit trail Suspension is a boolean with no recorded reason. Fine with one admin; indefensible with three.
  • Fraud signals Disposable email domains, high refund rate, chargebacks, many accounts from one address.
  • Per-host sending limits Every host currently sends on your domain's reputation. One bad list degrades deliverability for everyone.
  • Bounce visibility for hosts A host whose registrants are bouncing thinks their reminders are working. They are not, and only you can see it.
  • Watchlist Accounts to keep an eye on without acting yet.

Growth and lifecycle

Acting on what the data shows Medium 7 additions
  • Affiliate management and payout queuetables exist Approve affiliates, see confirmed balances above the $50 threshold, mark payouts paid. Nobody can be paid today — the tables are written to and never read.
  • Saved segments “Free accounts with a published webinar who have not upgraded” is the best upsell list the business has, and there is no way to produce it.
  • Broadcast to a segment Email a segment from the panel, using the platform templates that already exist.
  • Targeted announcements Announcements are all-or-nothing. Most are relevant to one plan or one cohort.
  • Onboarding funnel Which of the six setup steps people abandon. The checklist already computes completion per user; nothing aggregates it.
  • Feedback and NPS viewer Needed the moment the feedback email starts sending.
  • Churn reasons Ask at cancellation and aggregate the answers. Otherwise churn is a number with no story.

Admin governance

Fine with exactly one admin. Each of these becomes necessary the day there are two.

Controlling the controllers At two admins 7 additions
  • A proper admin audit log Plan grants are currently recorded in impersonation_logs with a reason string. That was expedient, and it is the wrong table.
  • Impersonation log viewertable exists Every session is recorded with who, whom, why and for how long. Nothing displays it, which defeats most of the point of recording it.
  • Admin roles Support, finance and engineering need different powers. Today is_admin grants all of them.
  • Enforced two-factor for admins One compromised admin password currently reaches every customer record on the platform.
  • Force sign-out a user Suspension stops the next sign-in. It does not end a session already open.
  • Maintenance mode A banner and a pause on session starts, for the migration you cannot do live.
  • IP allowlist on /superadmin Optional, and cheap insurance on the highest-value surface in the product.

The panel itself

Usability, including one real bug Includes a bug 9 additions
  • Pagination on the user list A bug, not a feature. The query is capped at 200 rows with no paging — customer 201 is invisible, and nothing says so.
  • Sortable columns Newest, most webinars, highest revenue, last active.
  • Bulk actions Grant, suspend or message a selection rather than one row at a time.
  • Command palette Jump to a customer or a page without navigating. The fastest way to make a support tool feel fast.
  • Export any table The export machinery already exists from Phase 4 and 6, with the formula-injection guard.
  • Saved filters The three or four views you use daily, one click away.
  • Skeletons and empty states Built in Phase 9 and not yet applied here. The panel still shows bare spinners.
  • Mobile layout The tables scroll horizontally on a phone. You will read this panel on a phone.
  • Deltas on every metric A number without a comparison is not information. The stat tile already supports a delta.

What I'd build first

Ordered by value per day of work. The first two are mostly wiring — the data is already being written.

  1. The five orphaned tables

    Error viewer, feature flags, affiliate payouts, impersonation log, admin notes. The schema, writes and RLS are done — each needs a screen. Affiliate payouts is the urgent one: commission is accruing against people you have no way to pay.

  2. Global search and a user detail page

    Turns the panel from a dashboard into a support tool. Every support question starts by finding one customer, and right now that path ends at a table row.

  3. Pagination, sorting and CSV export

    Fixes the 200-row bug before it hides a real customer, and reuses export code that already exists.

  4. Platform health

    Cron monitor, queue depth, webhook failures, deliverability. Two silent failures this week each looked exactly like health; this is the screen that would have shown both.

  5. Activation funnel and cohort retention

    The two charts that tell you whether the business works, rather than only what it billed. Both are derivable from data already stored.

  6. Refunds, trial extensions and credits

    With Phase 7 billing live, these are the concessions support makes constantly. Ships alongside the first real Stripe keys.

Counts verified against the codebase: five pages under app/superadmin; error_logs, feature_flags, affiliates, affiliate_referrals and admin_note have no references in any superadmin page, component or API route; the user query is capped at .limit(200) with no pagination.
